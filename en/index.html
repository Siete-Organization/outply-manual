User guide

How to use Outply

Outply sends cold email from your clients' mailboxes, paced so the domain doesn't get burned, and tells you who replied. This guide walks through the tool screen by screen, in the order you use it.

01What Outply is

An email prospecting tool for agencies: several sequences running at once, for several clients, without any of them burning another's reputation.

Emails don't go out from a shared server: they go out from the client's own mailboxes (Gmail, Outlook or the client's own SMTP). The domain's reputation belongs to the client and isn't shared with anyone, which is why the tool takes so much care not to spend it.

The six pieces you need to understand

PieceWhat it is
WorkspaceA client. Its campaigns, contacts, mailboxes and metrics live inside it and never mix with another workspace's.
Mailbox (sending account)A real, connected email account, with its own daily limit, pace, sender name and signature.
CampaignA sequence: the steps a contact receives, the schedule, the mailbox pool and the sending caps.
StepOne touch in the sequence: an automatic email or a manual task (call, WhatsApp, LinkedIn).
ContactA person enrolled in a campaign, with their status and their next scheduled step.
PoolA campaign's mailboxes. Sends rotate among them (round-robin), and each mailbox splits its quota among the active campaigns that use it.

How an email goes out by default New

  • In plain text: no images (not even the signature's) and no open or click tracking. Each campaign can be switched to HTML (§06).
  • No unsubscribe link or footer: people who don't want more emails reply, and if they ask to unsubscribe they go onto the workspace blocklist automatically (§09).
  • Signed by the mailbox: the sender is the Display name of the mailbox that sends. The campaign has no sender of its own.
  • Follow-ups go in the same thread, with the subject “Re: ” plus step 1's subject.
  • With brakes on new mailboxes: a limit of 40 a day, 300 to 450 seconds between emails and a ramp that starts at 10 on the day the mailbox is added and rises by 3 each day.
What it does on its own

It stops the sequence as soon as someone replies (within minutes; up to 15 in Outlook). It blocks anyone who bounced across all clients, unless the rejection is about the reputation of the sending domain. At the close of each day, it pauses any campaign whose bounces exceed the threshold. And it spreads sends across mailboxes, campaigns and hours so they don't look like an automated burst.

02Signing in, choosing a workspace and finding your way around

Everything you see on screen belongs to the active workspace. If the numbers don't add up, first check which workspace is open in the top-left corner.

Sign in

  1. Open the Outply address you were given.

  2. Enter your email and password and click Sign in, or use Continue with Google.

  3. If you don't have an account, ask an agency admin to invite you: access is by invitation only.

Switch workspace

  1. Click the selector under the logo: it shows the client's name and its slug.

  2. Choose the workspace. The list is sorted A to Z; with more than seven, Search workspace… appears and searches by name or slug (if only one is left, Enter opens it). Outply takes you to its Overview.

If the selector says No workspaces assigned, nobody has given you access to any client yet: ask an agency admin.

Collapse the sidebar with the round button on its edge or with the [ key (outside a text field). Collapsed, only the icons remain, and Outply remembers it in that browser. On mobile, a header with icons for the first screens replaces the sidebar.

Interface language New

Outply is available in Spanish, English and Portuguese. The selector (ES, EN, PT) is at the bottom of the sidebar, when the sidebar is expanded, and it changes the screens and the server messages. The first time, it uses your browser's language; whatever you choose is remembered in that browser. Some agency screens, such as Visibility, and some buttons are still in Spanish.

Help: the “?” button New

At the bottom of the sidebar, next to the theme button, there is a question-mark icon (also when the sidebar is collapsed; on mobile, in the header). It opens this guide in a new tab, in the language your interface is set to. At the top of the guide there is a search box: press the / key to jump to it and type what you're looking for (bounce, ramp, queue).

The sidebar footer and the banners at the top

  • Status dot: API online in green, the engine is responding; API offline in red, what you see may be out of date. Next to it: the language, the “?” button and light or dark mode.
  • Your account: your initials, your name and the email you signed in with, so you don't work under the wrong account. Sign out ends the session after asking “Sign out of …?”.
  • New version: a banner tells you a new version has been published and this tab is still on the previous one. Click Reload when you finish what you were doing.
  • Outply is not responding: an amber banner says your change will be saved automatically as soon as it comes back. Don't close the window or reload.
  • Deliverability notices (agency admins only): a temporary amber banner about some domains. Got it hides it in that browser.

03Getting started

The shortest path from a new client to the first email sent. Each step has its own chapter later on.

  1. Create the client's workspace. Agency → Workspaces → New workspace. Only the name is required. An agency admin does this. See §17.

  2. Connect the mailboxes. Mailboxes → Connect. Use dedicated domains, never the client's main domain, and no more than three mailboxes per domain. Each mailbox needs a Display name. See §04.

  3. Check authentication. Deliverability: SPF, DKIM, DMARC and MX in green. See §05.

  4. Send a delivery test from Deliverability → Send test to an address of yours on another provider, and check that it doesn't land in spam.

  5. Set up the campaign. Campaigns → New campaign opens the five-step wizard: Data, Sequence, Mailboxes, Contacts and Review. See §06.

  6. Load the contacts. In the wizard, one at a time; for a list, use Contacts → Import CSV with that campaign selected. A draft campaign accepts contacts and sends nothing. See §08.

  7. Activate with Activate campaign in the Review step, or later with Activate on the campaign page. If anything is missing, the tool tells you everything at once.

  8. Check the queue. Send queue shows who goes out today, at what time and from which mailbox. See §07.

New mailboxes come with the brakes on, and that's fine

A newly connected mailbox comes with a ramp by default (10 emails on the day it's added and 3 more each day), a wait of 300 to 450 seconds between emails and a limit of 40 a day. You don't need to turn anything on. Each client's cap is decided by their deliverability team: don't raise it on your own to go faster. See §19.

04Sending mailboxes

Mailboxes is the list of connected email accounts: how much each one sends today, whether they're healthy, how fast they write and what name they sign with.

Connect a mailbox

  1. Click Connect and choose gmail, outlook or SMTP.

  2. Gmail or Outlook (recommended): Authorize with Google or Authorize with Microsoft, grant the permissions and you're brought back automatically. The account name comes from the provider as the Display name.

  3. In Gmail, tick every box, as the dialog reminds you. The essential one is “Read, compose, send, and permanently delete all your email from Gmail”: without it, Outply doesn't add the mailbox and you come back to a red notice. Try again with it ticked.

  4. SMTP: email, Display name, SMTP server and port (usually 587), user, password, and IMAP server and port (usually 993). IMAP is required: without it, replies aren't detected. Connecting checks both.

Outlook doesn't need IMAP, but its replies can take up to 15 minutes to appear. If an SMTP mailbox's password was changed, go back to Connect → SMTP with the same email: the same mailbox is updated, with its history. Agency admins only: a notice about the Google account quota may appear above the table; once it runs out, no more Gmail mailboxes can be added.

Reading the table

Pause, activate and remove

  • Pausar (Pause) takes the mailbox out of rotation and empties its queue: anything scheduled is cancelled and those contacts move to the other mailboxes. It's also offered on mailboxes in error.
  • Activar (Activate) only appears on a paused mailbox, and it checks the permission first: if that fails, it doesn't activate it and shows the reason.
  • Dar de baja (Remove mailbox) removes the mailbox from the client: its capacity stops counting, its queue is cancelled and it disappears from the list; the history is kept. It asks for confirmation; to get it back you have to connect it again. Workspace admins or agency admins only (for anyone else the button returns an error).
How to recover a mailbox in error

Go back to Connect and authorize again (for SMTP, with the new password); changing its status doesn't renew the permission. Common causes: an expired permission, a Gmail box left unticked, a changed SMTP password or a deleted Google account. If the provider blocks the account, Outply pauses it automatically with the reason in red. In every case, its queue is released at the first failure and those contacts go out through other mailboxes. See §20.

Mailbox settings

Settings on its row. Nothing is saved until you click Guardar ajustes (Save settings).

General

  • Display name: the name the recipient sees, and the value of {{SenderName}}. An active mailbox without a name prevents activating the campaigns that use it.
  • Daily limit: 40 by default. Above 50 the tool warns you: providers look at how the account behaves, not at what you configure. If you lower it, anything already scheduled that no longer fits moves to the following days.
  • Sending domain (agency admins only): which daily domain cap the mailbox counts against, or No domain (doesn't count against any cap).

Human pace (jitter between sends)

  • Minimum (seconds) and Maximum (seconds): each email waits a random time in that range; 300 to 450 by default. With that, a mailbox fits about 86 emails into a 9-hour window (108 at most): the wait is also a cap. Raising it reschedules what's already scheduled.
  • Maximum emails per minute: the provider's ceiling (Outlook cuts off at around 30). Leave it at No cap if you don't need it. The screen tells you whether this cap or your minimum wait is the one that applies.

Warmup: actually a ramp

Despite the name, it doesn't send warm-up emails: it only limits how many go out while the mailbox is new. It comes on (Warmup on), with Start per day at 10 and Increase per day at 3. Days count from when the mailbox was added: 10 that day, 13 the next, and so on up to the daily limit; with 40, it reaches the cap after ten days. It no longer holds back an older mailbox. Warm-up with an external network of mailboxes is something else, and it's turned off (§17).

Signature

  1. Write the signature (HTML or text), or click Subir imagen (Upload image) to add a PNG, JPG, GIF or WEBP of up to 300 KB.

  2. Adjust the Image width with the slider.

  3. Look at Preview · actual size: the box is 600 px wide, the width of an email. If the image spills over the sides, it will in Gmail too.

  4. Click Guardar ajustes. The signature goes at the end of every email from that mailbox.

The image only travels in HTML campaigns

In HTML, the image is embedded in the email and doesn't depend on the email client unblocking it. In plain text, the default format, it doesn't travel: the signature goes out as text, and a signature that is only an image doesn't go out at all.

05Deliverability

If emails land in spam, no other screen matters. This is where you check that the domains are set up correctly.

Workspace domains

Each card is a sending domain, with a score from 0 to 100 and four checks. When one fails, what to do appears below it.

CheckWhat it checks
SPFThat the domain authorizes that server to send on its behalf.
DKIMThat emails are signed and can't be forged.
DMARCWhat the receiving server should do if something doesn't match.
MXThat the domain can receive email. Without MX, replies are lost.

Below is the domain quota: what goes out from it today against its daily cap. That cap overrides the mailboxes' caps, but by default it's so high it doesn't slow anything down (you'll see something like 35/1000000): what really limits sending is each mailbox's cap. The agency can lower it to slow down a specific domain; it isn't changed from this screen.

Generate the DNS records

  1. Type the domain in the DNS records panel.

  2. Choose the provider: Google, Microsoft or Custom, and click Generate.

  3. Copy each value and send them to whoever manages the DNS. Changes take time to propagate: come back later to see the checks in green.

Postmaster and delivery test

Postmaster looks up the reputation Google gives the domain and its spam rate; it requires the domain to be verified in Google Postmaster Tools. Send test sends a real email from the mailbox you choose to the address you type (preferably on another provider). Do it with every new mailbox before adding it to a pool.

Blocklist monitoring New

Every day, Outply checks whether the sending domains appear on the most widely used public blocklists (SURBL, Spamhaus DBL, URIBL and Sophos) and alerts the agency on Slack when one is added or removed. It isn't shown on this screen. To find out whether a specific email lands in the inbox, spam or promotions, an agency admin can run a placement test (§17).

06Campaigns

A campaign is a sequence with its own schedule, mailboxes and caps. It's the screen where you spend the most time.

The campaign list

Active campaigns come first and, within each group, the one with the most recent Last activity. Search campaigns by name… filters as you type and Per page chooses 50 or 100 (remembered in that browser). The window column also shows the sending days, and the copy icon on each row duplicates the campaign.

Activate or pause several at once New

  1. Tick the campaigns' boxes, or the header box for the whole page.

  2. In the bar that appears, click Activate or Pause and confirm.

  3. Any that couldn't be changed stay ticked, with the reason (for example, one is missing configuration). Clear removes the selection.

Create a campaign: the wizard New

New campaign opens Set up a campaign: five steps in the order you need them, each with an explanation. Next is never blocked: if something is missing, an amber notice says so and lets you continue; only activating requires everything. Back returns to the previous step. The campaign is created when you finish step 1, and from then on Save and exit leaves it as a Draft so you can finish it from the campaign page, which has the same panels.

Step 1 · Data

  • Name: only you see it; the recipient never reads it.
  • Time zone: the one the window is read in. Below it, the current time there.
  • From / To: the sending window; 09:00 to 18:00 to start with.
  • Sending days: the day boxes L M X J V S D (Monday to Sunday; X is Wednesday), Monday to Friday to start with. Anything due on another day waits for the next ticked day. At least one is required.
  • Tracking domain (optional): the client's subdomain for measuring opens and clicks, such as trk.tudominio.com. New campaigns go out in plain text and don't measure anything, so it's only used if you switch the campaign to HTML.

Click Create and continue.

Steps 2, 3 and 4 · Sequence, Mailboxes and Contacts

Step 2 is the Steps panel and step 3 the Mailbox pool; both are explained below. In step 4 you can enroll contacts one at a time with Enroll. Its text mentions importing a CSV, but that button isn't in this panel: click Save and exit, go to Contacts → Import CSV with this campaign selected and come back to it to activate it. It can also be activated without contacts: it starts sending as soon as the first one comes in.

Step 5 · Review

A summary (Sequence steps, Mailboxes in the pool, Contacts in the campaign, Format and Tracking domain) and the same check that runs at launch. Activate campaign can only be clicked if nothing is missing in red; Leave it as a draft takes you to the campaign page without activating it.

The campaign has no sender

Each email is signed with the Display name of the mailbox that sends it: with a pool of three people, each one signs with their own name. That's why an active mailbox in the pool without a name prevents activating the campaign.

The campaign page New

At the top, the name (the pencil opens Rename campaign), the current time in its time zone and the Activate or Pause, Duplicate and Delete buttons. Below, Emails sent, Replies, Bounces and Reply rate: these count emails, not people. The rest is in three tabs:

TabWhat it contains
OverviewNotices about what's missing, the steps with their variants, the pool and the settings.
ContactsThis campaign's contacts, with a search box (§08).
Send queueWho gets an email from this campaign today (§07).

Duplicate creates a copy with steps, variants, settings and pool, without contacts, as a draft, named “X (copia)” and with the same format as the original.

What's missing before launch

In the Overview tab, above the steps, there are two panels:

  • In red, This is missing before it can be activated (or This is holding the campaign back, if it's already active): there are no steps; an email step has no subject or content; the pool is empty or no mailbox can send; some active mailbox in the pool has no sender name (the notice says which); there are no sending days.
  • In amber, It can send, but it's worth reviewing: a mailbox in the pool can't send and its share is spread across the others; a mailbox with no per-minute cap could go over 30 per minute; a variable will come out empty for some of the contacts who still have emails to receive; sends failed in the last 24 hours (with the most frequent reason).

When you click Activate it checks again. With anything in red it doesn't start (The campaign is not set up yet); with only amber, you choose between Review again and Activate anyway.

Sequence steps

  1. In the Steps panel, click Step.

  2. Channel: email sends automatically; call, whatsapp, linkedin and manual create a task in Tasks with the instruction you write. It can't be changed later.

  3. Condition (email only): always, or depending on whether the contact opened or clicked the previous email (if opened, if not opened, if clicked, if not clicked).

  4. Wait (days): step 1 is set to 0 and goes out on enrollment. The others count from when the previous email went out, not from when you enrolled the contact.

  5. Subject and body: the body is written in HTML; in plain text it's converted when sending (links appear as their address and images disappear).

  6. Click Preview and then Add step.

Three things worth knowing

The follow-ups' subject is “Re: ” plus step 1's subject, because they go in the same thread. Steps 2 and later still ask for a subject, but it isn't the one the contact will see.
Without tracking (plain text or no tracking domain) nobody “opens” or “clicks”: if opened and if clicked are never met, and their opposites always are.
Changing a step's wait only affects contacts who haven't reached it yet.

Editor drafts New

What you write in a step or a variant is saved in the browser. If you reload or close without saving, when you come back you see “You have unsaved changes in this step, from …” with Restore and Discard. It only lives in that browser. If Outply doesn't respond when you save, an amber banner tells you and the save is retried automatically: don't close the window.

Personalize the email

Contact data goes between double curly braces, and it doesn't matter how you write it: {{FirstName}}, {{first_name}} and {{ First Name }} are the same field.

{{referred_by}}The contact's Referred by field.

If you use a variable that doesn't exist, an amber notice tells you: it would come out empty (“Hi ,”). Below the editor, Insert contact field pastes the field where your cursor is.

Plain text or HTML New

You choose it per campaign with the Plain text switch in the settings. It's on for new campaigns; campaigns created before this change stay in HTML.

Switch to HTML only if you need images or tracking: the signal that counts is the reply. And a tracking domain only measures if it really points to Outply (whoever manages the client's domain has to connect it); otherwise the email still goes out but without tracking, and the screen doesn't warn you.

Preview

It builds the email with a real contact from the campaign (or a sample one) and the signature of a mailbox in the pool, in plain text if the campaign is. If a field is empty for that contact, it tells you and suggests a fallback. It's the only way to see the email as it arrives.

A/B variants

  1. Click + Variant under an email step.

  2. Keep the Label it suggests (B, C…) and set its % of traffic. The split is applied exactly, not at random.

  3. Write the subject and body, with the same helpers as in the step.

  4. Compare them in Analytics → Breakdown by step and variant.

A is the control: the step's own subject and body. You edit it with the step's pencil, and it gets whatever percentage you didn't give to the variants; with 100% allocated, it stops being sent. In follow-ups a variant only changes the body, because the subject is the “Re:” of step 1's control, also for contacts who received another variant.

Mailbox pool

Click one of the mailboxes shown with a + to add it; the trash can removes it. Sends rotate in order of position, and a red dot marks a mailbox in error. Without at least one active mailbox, the campaign can't be activated. Remember that each mailbox's quota is split among all the active campaigns that use it: a mailbox of 40 in four campaigns gives about 10 to each (§18).

Settings

The Settings panel in the Overview tab. Nothing is saved until you click Guardar ajustes.

If new contacts plus follow-ups exceed the total, the field hint warns in amber and says follow-ups go first. That isn't the case: the quota is split in proportion to the two caps, and new contacts go first within their share (§18). Auto-pause is decided at the close of each campaign day (midnight in its time zone): with at least the minimum sample in the window and the rate above the threshold, the campaign is paused and its queue is emptied.

Activate, pause and delete

  • Activate: starts sending within its window, after checking that nothing is missing.
  • Pause: stops sending and empties the queue; the quota goes back to the other campaigns that share mailboxes. When you reactivate it, its contacts get send times again.
Delete can't be undone

It deletes the campaign with its steps, contacts and sends. If you only want it to stop sending, use Pause.

07Send queue New

Who gets an email today, at what time and from which mailbox. And, when needed, change the order: bring someone forward to today or take them out of today.

It's in the menu, Prospecting → Send queue, and in the tab of the same name on each campaign. The screen has two views: Send queue, with a dropdown for All campaigns or a single one; and By campaign, one row per active campaign with Next send, Sent today, Pending today, Last one today, Waiting and Upcoming (click a row to see its queue).

Reading the queue

One row per email, in the order they go out: Time, Contact, Campaign (in the all-campaigns view), Step and Mailbox. Times are in your local time; hover to see the campaign's.

SectionWhat's there
Earlier · N sent · M lockedCollapsed: what already went out today and what goes out in less than an hour, with a padlock. That can't be moved.
Today's rowsToday's emails that can still be moved.
End of todayThe line where the sending day ends.
Tomorrow · NWhat already has a time on later days. Read-only.
Waiting · NContacts who are due and don't have a time yet, in the order they'll get one: whoever has waited longest goes first.
Upcoming · NContacts who aren't due yet, with the date from which they will be. Read-only.

A long list in Waiting isn't an error: it's what didn't fit today. It isn't lost (§18).

Reorder the queue

  1. Click Edit. The queue freezes and you work on a draft: nothing reaches the server until you save.

  2. Drag rows by the handle on the left. If you tick several and drag one, they all move.

  3. With rows ticked you also have To the top, To the end, Remove from today and Clear selection. Dropping on the End of today line means to the end; dropping in Waiting means remove from today.

  4. Undo reverts the last change; Cancel discards them all.

  5. Click Save. It's all or nothing: if a move is no longer valid (for example, that email has just gone out), none is saved and a red strip says which one and why.

The rules of the game

  • Neither times nor mailboxes change, only who fills each slot: if you put C ahead of A, C goes out at the time and through the mailbox A had.
  • Adding someone to today pushes the queue: that campaign's last email today goes back to waiting. Removing someone from today gives the slot to the first person waiting in that campaign.
  • Anything going out in less than an hour can't be moved.
  • Each campaign allocates its own times: in the all-campaigns view, a contact can only take slots from their own campaign (the other rows dim while you drag).
  • Caps: the campaign's maximum new contacts per day doesn't stop you bringing someone forward; the workspace's maximum new contacts per company does. Nor can anyone get in who is on the blocklist or doesn't meet their step's condition.

Everyone can view the queue. Operators and admins can save (for a viewer it returns an error).

08Contacts

Contacts shows who is enrolled in each campaign, which step they're on and what comes next for them. You choose the campaign in the top-right corner: everything you do here affects its contacts.

Import a CSV

  1. Choose the target campaign (a draft one works) and click Import CSV.

  2. Select the file. The tool reads the headers and suggests a mapping.

  3. Check the Column mapping: each header goes to Email, Nombre (First name), Apellido (Last name), Empresa (Company), a Personalizado (Custom) field or Ignorar (Ignore).

  4. Choose Enter at step (usually 1) and look at the Sample (first rows) in case a column is shifted.

09Replies

Who replied and what they said, in a single list.

Only replies from people appear here. Automatic out-of-office replies mark the contact as away, and bounces have their own screen (§10): neither counts toward the reply rate. Unsubscribing by reply New

Emails carry no unsubscribe link: people who don't want more emails reply. If the reply asks for it explicitly, Outply adds their address to the workspace blocklist with the reason unsubscribe, and no other campaign for that client writes to them again. You don't have to do anything.

  • Counts as unsubscribing: “unsubscribe”, “remove me”, “take me off”, “do not contact”, their Spanish and Portuguese equivalents (“dame de baja”, “descadastrar”…), or a reply that is just “no” (“no thanks”, “stop”). Only what the contact wrote is checked, not the quoted email.
  • Doesn't count: “not interested”. If the client wants to try again later, that's your call.

For the campaign it's just another reply: the contact is marked Replied.

You reply from the mailbox

Outply detects the reply and stops the sequence within minutes (up to 15 in Outlook). The conversation continues in Gmail, Outlook or wherever the mailbox lives. What happens to that person in other campaigns in the workspace depends on the When someone replies setting (§17).

10Bounces New

Why each email bounced, with the code and the full notice from the recipient's server. These are exactly the bounces that count toward the rate: you can count and check them one by one.

Each bounce shows the person, their company, when it happened, the campaign, the step and the mailbox; a Duro (Hard) or Blando (Soft) badge; the code (for example, 550 · 5.1.1) with a one-sentence reading; the diagnostic as is; and See the full DSN, the whole notice as it reached the mailbox. The dropdown filters by campaign. Some older bounces say the reason wasn't saved: the notice is still in the mailbox that received it, as an email from MAILER-DAEMON.

How to read the code

Starts withReading on screen and what it usually means
5.1.“The mailbox doesn't exist: closed or misspelled”. The list is getting old.
5.2.“Mailbox full or disabled”. Abandoned accounts.
5.4.“The recipient's server isn't responding”.
5.7.“Rejected by policy or sender reputation”. The problem is the sender: if it repeats, stop and review the domain.
Other 5.“Permanent rejection”.
4.“Temporary failure: retried in 24 h”. That text is outdated: nothing is retried, and any bounce is final the first time.

What Outply does with a bounce

Any bounce, permanent or temporary, is final the first time: the contact becomes Bounced, their sequence stops, it counts toward the campaign's rate and the address goes onto the workspace blocklist and the global one, so no client writes to them again. There are three exceptions:

  • Rejection because of the sending domain's reputation. The recipient rejects because of the sender's domain or IP, not because of the address. It counts as a bounce in its campaign, but the address doesn't go onto any blocklist.
  • The sending account is blocked. It isn't the contact's bounce: the contact isn't touched and it doesn't count toward the rate. Outply pauses the mailbox with the reason in red in Mailboxes.
  • The recipient's forwarding was blocked. Their mailbox forwards to another address and their organization blocks it; the original already reached them. It's ignored.

The Blando badge only appears on bounces from before October 2026; today every new bounce is Duro. Below, Unattributed collects notices that don't match any send: they don't count toward the rate. If many appear, tell your agency admin: the rate would be coming out too low.

18How Outply decides what goes out

Why less is sometimes sent than you expected. Four levels, in this order.

Level 1 Each mailbox's cap

Each mailbox has its cap for today: its daily limit or, while the ramp lasts, the ramp's. The sum of the caps is the absolute ceiling for the day: three mailboxes at 40 make 120 emails. The quota counts what was sent plus what is scheduled for today. The domain cap overrides its mailboxes' caps, but by default it doesn't slow anything down.

↓
Level 2 The split between campaigns

Each mailbox's quota is divided equally among the active campaigns that use it: a mailbox of 40 in four campaigns gives 10 to each. Whatever one campaign doesn't use is offered to the same client's other campaigns that have a queue (it never leaves the client), and they take turns so it doesn't always go to the same ones. A campaign that is activated, or receives contacts, when the mailboxes are already booked gets its share back the same day, little by little, from the campaigns running above theirs. On top of that, each campaign respects its Total sends / day.

↓
Level 3 New contacts and follow-ups

The campaign's quota is split between new contacts and follow-ups in proportion to their two caps: with 20 and 60, a quarter is for new contacts. New contacts go first within their share; follow-ups use theirs plus whatever new contacts didn't fill, and anything left over goes back to new contacts. With neither cap set, it's half and half; with one at 0, the other takes everything. The workspace's maximum new contacts per company also applies here.

↓
Level 4 Each email, one by one

It only goes out within the window and on sending days; outside them, or if it was delayed by a stoppage, it waits for the next opening. Each email respects its mailbox's wait (300–450 seconds by default) and its per-minute cap, and the pool's mailboxes take turns. The next step is counted from when the email was actually sent: if step 1 went out late, so does step 2.

An email without quota isn't lost

It stays in Waiting (you can see it in the Send queue) and goes out as soon as there's room; within new contacts or follow-ups, whoever has waited longest goes first. A send is never cancelled for lack of quota. Only when a campaign gets its share back do some emails from other campaigns go back to waiting: the ones with the latest times, never the ones about to go out.

Where to look: your campaign's Send queue, the Quota today column in Mailboxes and, if you're an agency admin, Visibility.

19Best practices

The rules for setting up a client in Outply. They're the values mailboxes and campaigns are created with, and they're behind the notices you'll see on screen.

The golden rule of domains

The client's main domain is never used for cold email. You send from dedicated variant domains (trytucliente.com, gettucliente.com): if one gets damaged, it doesn't drag down the main domain or the others.

The default values

MeasureValueWhy
Emails per mailbox per day40The limit every mailbox starts with. Each client's cap is decided by their deliverability team; above 50, the tool warns you.
Mailboxes per domain3Spreads the risk: a burned domain takes three mailboxes with it, not ten.
Wait between emails300–450 sRandom, the way a person writes.
New mailbox ramp10 + 3/dayGoing from zero to forty emails at once is exactly what filters look for.
Bounce threshold3%Over 7 days and with at least 50 sends. Once it's exceeded, the campaign pauses automatically before the damage gets expensive.
FormatPlain textIt looks like an email written by hand, with no pixels or rewritten links.

Raising the daily limit doesn't make more emails go out: providers look at how the account behaves, and the wait and the split also limit sending. For more volume, add more mailboxes.

Before launching

  • SPF, DKIM, DMARC and MX in green.
  • A Display name with a person's name on every mailbox in the pool.
  • The ramp on for every new mailbox (it comes that way) and no synthetic warm-up.
  • One delivery test per mailbox, to an inbox on another provider.
  • A preview of step 1 with a real contact.
  • A reply-to-unsubscribe line at the end of every email, instead of a link.

While it runs

  • Check Overview every morning: the notice “One mailbox is not sending” means the campaigns that depend on it are sending less than you think.
  • Check Bounces: lots of 5.1. mean the list is old; a repeated 5.7. means you need to stop and review the domain.
  • Read the replies even if there are few: they're the only signal that can't be faked.
  • Limit new contacts per company per day: five emails to the same company on the same Tuesday is the fastest way to get marked as spam.
  • Each active campaign takes its share of the mailboxes: pause the ones that have nobody left to write to.
  • Let A/B variants build up sends before you choose: with twenty emails per variant there's nothing to read.

20Troubleshooting

The most common questions, with the real cause and what to do.

I can't activate the campaign

Why Something is missing: steps, a subject or content in an email step, mailboxes that can send, a sender name on some mailbox in the pool, or sending days.

What to do The This is missing before it can be activated panel lists them all. For mailboxes without a name, set a Display name in Mailboxes → Settings.

“One mailbox is not sending” on the overview

Why It's in Error or the provider blocked the account: an expired permission, a Gmail box left unticked, a changed SMTP password or a deleted Google account. The reason shows in red under its email, in Mailboxes.

What to do Reconnect it with Connect (in Gmail, with every box ticked; in SMTP, with the same email and the new password). Changing its status doesn't fix it. If the provider blocked the account, resolve that with the provider first.

Much less is being sent than I expected

Why It's almost always one of these brakes: a new mailbox's ramp; the wait between emails (about 86 a day at most in 9 hours); other campaigns using the same mailboxes; the campaign's caps or the new-contacts-per-company cap; the sending window or days; or mailboxes that are down.

What to do Look at the Send queue and the Quota today column in Mailboxes; an agency admin sees it all together in Visibility. Whatever didn't go out today isn't lost.

I activated a new campaign and almost nothing goes out today

Why It shares mailboxes with campaigns that had already booked the day. It's only entitled to its share and gets it back little by little. Without contacts, it sends nothing.

What to do Check that it has contacts and active mailboxes. For more volume, add mailboxes or pause campaigns that have nobody left to write to.

The email arrived with a gap: “Hi ,”

Why A variable with no data for that contact. It comes out empty, silently.

What to do Load or fix the data with Edit, or write a fallback: {{FirstName or 'ahí'}}. If it happens with many contacts, review the CSV mapping.

I don't see opens or clicks

Why Most likely, the campaign is in Plain text, which measures nothing. In HTML: tracking is off, there's no tracking domain, the domain doesn't point to Outply, or the workspace's tracking is off.

What to do Decide whether you really need to measure. If so: turn off Plain text, turn on tracking, confirm with whoever manages the domain that it's connected, and check Tracking on (opens and clicks) in the workspace settings.

The signature goes out without its image, or cut off

Why No image: the campaign is in plain text. Cut off: the image is wider than an email, and Gmail crops it.

What to do For the image, switch the campaign to HTML. If it's cut off, lower the Image width until it fits in the 600 px box.

The reply rate looks very low

Why You're probably reading the rate per email, which counts follow-ups.

What to do Look at the rate per contact in Analytics.

I imported a CSV and fewer contacts came in

Why Duplicates, suppressed contacts (on the workspace or global blocklist, for example because they bounced with another client) or invalid ones.

What to do Don't force the suppressed ones. For the invalid ones, the error list says which row failed.

Someone who already replied still gets emails from another campaign

Why Pausing on reply is set to Nothing for that workspace.

What to do In the workspace settings, set When someone replies to at least “That person, in your other campaigns”.

Someone asked to unsubscribe and I don't know if they were blocked

Why Only explicit requests count; “not interested” isn't an unsubscribe.

What to do Look for them in Blocklist → This workspace, with reason unsubscribe. If they aren't there and you want to block them, add them by hand.

A contact shows as Stopped and nobody touched it

Why Their address can't exist (and was blocked across all clients), or it went onto the blocklist when it already had an email scheduled.

What to do If the email was misspelled, add the contact again with the right one. If it's on the blocklist, find out why before removing it.

I stopped contacts by mistake

Why Stop is final, and in the campaign's Contacts tab it doesn't ask for confirmation.

What to do It can't be undone: enroll them again or import them again. For temporary holds, use Pause.

When I save the queue it says it can't

Why Saving is all or nothing and a move was no longer valid: the email goes out in less than an hour, the contact got a send time while you were editing, it's on the blocklist or its company reached its cap.

What to do The red strip says which one. Click Cancel, then Edit again, and redo whatever still makes sense.

A campaign paused itself

Why At the close of the day, its bounce rate over the window went above the threshold, with the minimum sample met.

What to do Look at that campaign's Bounces: addresses that don't exist call for cleaning the list; reputation rejections point to the domain.

I see “Restricted access”, or Visibility shows me no data

Why These are agency admin screens, with data from all workspaces.

What to do If you need them, ask an agency admin for the permission. For your campaign, use the Send queue and Mailboxes.

The indicator says “API offline”

Why The browser can't reach Outply's engine, often because of an update.

What to do Wait a minute and reload. If it stays red, don't make decisions based on what you see, and tell the technical team. If you see the amber saving banner, don't close the window.

21Glossary

Mailbox
A real email account connected to Outply, from which emails are sent.
Unsubscribe by reply
A reply asking not to receive more emails. It puts the address on the workspace blocklist.
Draft
A campaign that hasn't been activated yet. It accepts contacts and sends nothing.
Synthetic warm-up
Automatic emails among a network of mailboxes to “warm up” an account. It's turned off. Not to be confused with the ramp.
Send queue
Who gets an email today, at what time and from which mailbox, plus what's waiting and what's coming.
Control (variant A)
The step's own subject and body: the version the variants compete against.
Quota
The emails a mailbox or a domain can send today. It counts what was sent plus what is scheduled for today.
Display name
The mailbox name the recipient sees. It's the sender.
Tracking domain
A subdomain of the client's (trk.tudominio.com) for measuring opens and clicks. Only in HTML campaigns, and only if it points to Outply.
DSN
The bounce notice the recipient's server sends back, with the code and the reason.
Waiting
Contacts who are due an email and didn't fit today. They aren't lost.
Enroll
To put a contact into a campaign, at a specific step.
Workspace
A client, with its campaigns, contacts, mailboxes and metrics isolated from the rest.
Jitter
The random wait between two emails from the same mailbox. 300–450 seconds by default.
Blocklist / suppression
Addresses and domains that never receive email. The global one blocks in all workspaces and fills itself with bounces.
Minimum sample / Window
The safeguards of the bounce rule: a campaign isn't judged until it has 50 sends in the last 7 days.
OOO
An automatic out-of-office reply. It doesn't count as a reply and the sequence continues.
Step
Each touch in the sequence: an automatic email or a manual task.
Pool
The mailboxes assigned to a campaign.
Placement test
Sending step 1 to test inboxes at Gmail, Outlook and Yahoo to see whether it lands in the inbox, spam or promotions.
Ramp
The cap that rises each day on a new mailbox: 10 on the day it's added and 3 more each day. On screen, “Warmup”.
Bounce
An email returned by the recipient's server. Final the first time; it blocks the address across all clients unless it's a reputation rejection.
Reputation rejection
A bounce caused by the sender's domain or IP. It counts in the campaign, but the address isn't blocked.
Split
How a mailbox's quota is divided among the active campaigns that use it.
Round-robin
The rotation of sends among the pool's mailboxes.
SPF · DKIM · DMARC
The three DNS records that prove your emails are legitimate.
Spintax
Several wordings for one fragment: {hola|buenas}. Each email picks one.
Plain text
The default format: text only, no images and no open or click tracking.
Variable / merge field
A piece of contact data inserted in the email: {{FirstName}}.
Sending window
The time range and the days when the campaign can send.
Webhook
An automatic notification Outply sends to another system when an event happens.
